Is Your Med Spa's Ad Spend Actually Converting?

A guide for med spa and aesthetic clinic owners running Facebook or Instagram ads.

Before-and-after posts are getting engagement. Leads are coming through Instagram. But aesthetic treatments are a visual, impulse-driven purchase — nobody wakes up and searches "Botox near me" the way they search for an emergency plumber — which means the metrics that matter for a med spa's ad account look different from almost any other business on this site.

Per 2026 paid ad benchmarks for aesthetic clinics, high-performing med spa Meta campaigns see cost per lead between $50 and $100, with average campaigns ranging $75 to $200. In-platform Instant Form leads on Meta tend to run cheaper — often $15 to $50 — than leads sent to a website landing page, since there's less friction to complete the form.

This guide covers how to check Facebook and Instagram ROI for a med spa, why in-platform lead forms and website conversions need to be evaluated differently, and how to calculate a true cost per booked client.

Why Med Spa Lead Quality Varies So Much by Form Type

Meta's native Instant Forms — where someone fills out a form without ever leaving Facebook or Instagram — generally produce a lower cost per lead than sending traffic to your website. But that lower cost often comes with a trade-off: the ease of a pre-filled, in-app form can mean less-motivated inquiries compared to someone who clicked through to an actual booking page.

That means comparing an Instant Form campaign's cost per lead directly against a landing-page campaign's cost per lead isn't a fair comparison — the two produce genuinely different lead quality, and need to be judged by what share of each actually converts to a booked, paying appointment.

How to Calculate Your True Cost Per Med Spa Client

Step 1: Separate leads by source — Instant Form vs. website landing page — since blending them into one cost-per-lead number obscures which is actually working.

Step 2: Total your ad spend and agency management fee for the campaign period.

Step 3: Divide by booked, kept appointments for each source separately, not raw leads or form fills.

Step 4: Compare against average client value — including repeat-treatment value where relevant, since many aesthetic treatments involve a return visit or package rather than a single purchase.

Spend + fee ÷ Kept appointments = True cost per client

Video creative — treatment demonstrations, real patient testimonials — consistently outperforms static images for aesthetic advertising, and higher-intent lead forms that include qualifying questions ("which treatment are you interested in," "what's your timeline") tend to reduce cost per booked consultation compared to a bare "request more info" form.

What to Check If Leads Look Cheap But Aren't Booking

  • Is the campaign using Instant Forms, or driving to a landing page? A cheap Instant Form lead isn't directly comparable to a landing-page lead — evaluate each by its own conversion rate, not a shared cost-per-lead number.
  • Does the lead form ask any qualifying questions? A bare "get more info" form tends to attract more casual, less committed inquiries than one that asks about treatment interest or timeline.
  • How fast is follow-up? Aesthetic leads are often impulse-driven — someone who saw a before-and-after and felt a moment of interest. That interest fades quickly without a prompt response.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's a good cost per lead for med spa Facebook ads?

Reported benchmarks generally place high-performing campaigns at $50-$100 per lead, with average campaigns running $75-$200. In-platform Instant Form leads often cost less ($15-$50) but tend to run lower intent than website landing-page leads, so cost per lead should be compared within the same lead type, not across types.

Why does Instagram work better than Google Ads for med spas?

Aesthetic treatments tend to be a visual, discovery-driven purchase rather than a search-driven one — most people aren't actively searching for a specific treatment the way they'd search for an emergency repair. Instagram and Facebook's visual, interest-based targeting are built to generate that kind of demand rather than only capture existing search intent.

Should I use Instant Forms or send traffic to my website?

Both have a place. Instant Forms generally produce a lower cost per lead and can work well for broad awareness offers, while a website landing page tends to attract more qualified, higher-intent inquiries at a higher cost per lead. Comparing true cost per booked appointment for each — not just cost per lead — shows which is earning its budget for your specific offers.

Does creative format really affect cost per lead this much?

Yes — video content, especially real treatment demonstrations and patient testimonials, has been reported to meaningfully outperform static images for aesthetic advertising specifically, likely because the visual, procedural nature of these treatments benefits from motion and real results more than a still photo can convey.

Is this the same calculation as the general Facebook Ads guide?

The underlying formula matches the general Facebook Ads breakdown — spend plus fee, divided by paying customers, compared to customer value — but med spas need the added step of separating Instant Form leads from landing-page leads before comparing costs, since blending them produces a misleading average.

The Bottom Line

A med spa's Facebook and Instagram ad spend is working if true cost per booked, paying client — tracked separately for Instant Form and landing-page leads — stays comfortably below what a client is worth, including repeat-visit value where relevant. Raw cost per lead, especially blended across lead types, can be misleading in either direction.
