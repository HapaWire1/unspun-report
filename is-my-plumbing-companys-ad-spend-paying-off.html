Is Your Plumbing Company's Ad Spend Paying Off?

A guide for plumbing business owners running Google Ads, Local Service Ads, or both.

Emergency calls are coming in. Your ads look like they're generating leads. But plumbing has one of the widest cost-per-lead ranges in home services, and it's easy to be paying well above the going rate without realizing it — especially once you factor in what you're paying an agency to manage the account on top of the ad spend itself.

Per SearchLight Digital's Q1 2026 plumbing benchmark — tracking $14.6M in non-branded Google Ads spend across 524 plumbing contractors — the average cost per lead for non-branded plumbing search is $183. Branded search averages far less, and Performance Max sits around $72. The typical contractor in the dataset spends about $14,200/month across all trades, with roughly 40% allocated specifically to plumbing.

This guide covers how to check plumbing Google Ads ROI using your existing reports, why the emergency-driven nature of plumbing demand changes the math slightly compared to other trades, and how to land on a true cost per paying customer. Why Plumbing Economics Differ From Other Trades

Plumbing has a distinct advantage most other home service trades don't: demand is often emergency-driven. A burst pipe isn't a comparison-shopping moment — the homeowner searching "emergency plumber near me" at 11pm is ready to book, not researching quotes for next week. That urgency tends to produce higher conversion rates than most home services, even at a higher cost per lead.

That means a plumbing cost per lead that looks expensive next to, say, general contracting, isn't automatically a problem — it needs to be weighed against your book rate (what share of leads become paying jobs) and your average ticket, not compared directly to a cross-industry benchmark.

How to Calculate Your True Cost Per Plumbing Customer

Step 1: Isolate non-branded spend — this is the number that reflects real new-customer acquisition, not people who already knew your business name.

Step 2: Add your agency's management fee to the campaigns it's actually managing.

Step 3: Divide by booked jobs, not raw calls or form fills — a booked job is the only outcome that generates revenue.

Step 4: Weigh the result against your average ticket, keeping in mind that plumbing's emergency-driven demand often supports a higher acceptable cost per lead than other trades, provided your book rate holds up.

Spend + fee ÷ Booked jobs = True cost per customer

Response time matters more here than in almost any other trade covered on this site: a homeowner with an active leak is calling the next plumber on the list within minutes if the first one doesn't pick up. If your agency's reporting doesn't track how quickly leads get a callback, that's worth asking about directly — it affects book rate more than almost any other single factor.

Red Flags Specific to Plumbing Ad Accounts

  • Cost per lead reported without a job-category breakdown. Drain/sewer, water heater, and general plumbing calls can price very differently — a blended average can mask one category quietly losing money.
  • No visibility into response time. Given how emergency-driven plumbing demand is, a slow callback can turn an otherwise well-targeted, reasonably priced lead into a wasted one.
  • Branded and non-branded spend reported together. As with HVAC, this can make acquisition cost look artificially low.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's a good cost per lead for plumbing Google Ads?

Non-branded search — the campaign type acquiring genuinely new customers — has averaged around $167 to $183 per lead in recent industry data, notably higher than most other home service trades. That's not automatically bad; plumbing's emergency-driven demand tends to convert at higher rates, so the number to watch is cost per booked job compared to your average ticket, not cost per lead in isolation.

Why is plumbing more expensive per lead than HVAC or electrical?

Competition and search intent both play a role, but the emergency nature of plumbing demand is a major factor — advertisers are willing to pay more per click for searches that reliably convert quickly, which pushes up the auction price even as conversion rates stay strong.

Should I run Local Service Ads alongside standard Google Ads?

Many plumbing contractors do. LSAs (the "Google Guaranteed" listings) generally show lower cost per lead than standard search and only charge for leads within your service area and category, while standard search offers more control over messaging and targeting. Comparing true cost per booked job across both tells you where to shift budget.

How much does response time actually matter?

Significantly. Emergency-driven categories like plumbing see book rates drop sharply once a callback takes more than a few minutes, since the customer is often calling multiple providers at once. Fast response is one of the highest-leverage, lowest-cost improvements available to most plumbing ad accounts.

Is this calculation different from checking Google Ads ROI for other businesses?

The underlying math is identical — total spend divided by paying customers, compared to what a customer is worth — but plumbing's emergency-driven conversion rates mean a higher cost per lead can still be profitable in a way it might not be for a less urgent purchase.

The Bottom Line

"Is my plumbing company's ad spend paying off?" comes down to true cost per booked job — non-branded ad spend plus agency fee, divided by jobs actually booked — weighed against your average ticket and plumbing's naturally strong conversion rates.
